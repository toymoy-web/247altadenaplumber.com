A slab leak happens when a water line running underneath a concrete foundation develops a leak, and it needs a different diagnostic approach than a leak in an accessible wall or crawlspace.

Why Slab Leaks Happen In Altadena

Mid-century Altadena homes built between roughly 1955 and 1980 commonly run copper supply lines directly under the slab. Copper pipe has a working life, and once a pinhole opens from age or from a reaction with the surrounding soil, water can travel along the underside of the slab before surfacing somewhere else entirely.

Soil movement adds mechanical stress on top of that aging process. Altadena's mix of clay, sandy loam, and adobe soils shifts with seasonal moisture, and that movement can flex a rigid copper line resting against concrete until a small crack becomes a leak.

Electrolysis between copper pipe and certain soil conditions can also accelerate pinhole formation from the outside of the pipe, which is a separate mechanism from the gradual internal wear that eventually affects any copper supply line.

Signs You May Have A Slab Leak

Warm Patch On The Floor

A localized warm spot on tile or concrete flooring usually means a hot water line is leaking directly beneath it.

Unexplained Water Bill Increase

A slab leak runs continuously, so it often shows up as a steady, unexplained rise in usage.

Sound Of Running Water

Hearing water movement inside the floor or walls with every fixture off is a strong slab leak indicator.

Cracking In Flooring Or Baseboards

Moisture pushing up through a slab over time can cause tile cracking or baseboard damage along the leak's path.

How We Confirm A Slab Leak Without Cutting Concrete First

Acoustic listening equipment traces the sound of pressurized water escaping through the slab, which narrows the search to a specific section of floor before anything gets opened.

We isolate sections of the home's plumbing and pressure test each one to confirm which line is leaking, hot or cold side, before recommending a repair method. This step avoids cutting concrete in the wrong spot.

Any repair or reroute follows Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements for pipe material and support, whether the fix stays underground or moves the line through a wall or attic space.

Repair Approaches

Spot Repair Through The Slab

A single, well-located leak can sometimes be accessed by cutting a small section of concrete directly above it.

Reroute Above The Slab

Rerouting the line through a wall or attic avoids cutting concrete at all, often at a similar cost to a spot repair.

Repipe The Line

A home with more than one slab leak on aging copper is a stronger candidate for repiping the affected line entirely.

Common Questions

How much concrete has to be cut for a slab leak repair?

It depends on the method. A spot repair needs an opening only over the exact leak location, which acoustic detection narrows down first. A reroute avoids cutting the slab entirely.

Is a warm spot on my floor always a slab leak?

Not always, but it is one of the most reliable signs. Radiant heating systems can also cause warm spots, so we confirm with acoustic and pressure testing before assuming either cause.

Can a slab leak cause structural damage?

Left long enough, yes. Continuous moisture under a foundation can affect soil support and flooring, which is why early diagnosis matters.

Does homeowner's insurance cover slab leak repair?

Many policies cover the cost of accessing and repairing the leak itself, though coverage for the leak's cause versus resulting damage varies by policy. We recommend checking your specific coverage.

Before We Arrive

  • Note the exact location and size of any warm spot, and whether it is on the hot or cold side of the house relative to your water heater.
  • Check your water meter with everything off for at least an hour and record whether it moved. That reading helps confirm an active leak before the appointment.
  • Avoid placing rugs or furniture over a suspected warm spot, since covering it can trap heat and make the area harder to assess.
