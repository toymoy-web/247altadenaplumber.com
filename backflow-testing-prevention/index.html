Backflow prevention assemblies protect the public water supply from contamination, and California requires most properties with one to have it tested annually by a certified tester.

Why Backflow Testing Is Required In Altadena

California Code of Regulations Title 17 requires annual testing of backflow prevention assemblies by a certified tester, with results filed with the local water purveyor. In Altadena, that purveyor is whichever mutual water company serves your specific property.

Properties with an irrigation system, a fire sprinkler system, or certain commercial connections are the most common candidates for a required backflow assembly, since these connections carry a higher cross-contamination risk than a standard household supply line.

Because Title 17 gives local water purveyors room to set their own testing windows and notice procedures, the exact deadline and required paperwork can differ depending on which mutual water company issued the notice for your property.

When You Need Backflow Testing Or Service

Annual Testing Notice

Most water purveyors mail a notice when your assembly's annual test is due. Missing it can result in a compliance notice or service interruption.

New Assembly Installation

A new backflow assembly needs an initial test immediately after installation before it goes into service.

Visible Leaking Or Discharge

A backflow assembly that is leaking or discharging water continuously needs inspection, not just the annual test.

Failed Prior Test

An assembly that failed a previous test needs repair and retesting within the window your water purveyor specifies.

How Testing And Repair Work

Testing checks the assembly's check valves and relief mechanisms under controlled conditions to confirm it will actually stop backflow if a pressure drop occurs elsewhere in the system.

If an assembly fails, we repair or rebuild the specific failed component, then retest before filing the passing report with your water purveyor, all within the required compliance window.

Common Questions

How often does backflow testing have to happen?

California requires annual testing under Title 17, though your specific water purveyor's notice will confirm the exact deadline for your property.

What happens if I miss my backflow testing deadline?

Water purveyors typically issue a compliance notice first, but continued noncompliance can lead to service interruption in some jurisdictions.

Do I need a backflow assembly if I don't have irrigation?

Not necessarily. Standard residential connections without irrigation, fire suppression, or certain commercial features often do not require one, but your water purveyor's notice is the definitive source.

Who can legally perform a backflow test in California?

Only a California-certified backflow assembly tester. A visual inspection or a general handyman check does not satisfy the Title 17 requirement.

What is the difference between an RP and a DC assembly?

A reduced pressure (RP) assembly protects against both backpressure and backsiphonage and is required for higher-hazard connections. A double check (DC) assembly protects against lower-hazard connections. Your water purveyor's notice specifies which type your property needs.

Can I install my own backflow assembly?

Installation should be done by a licensed plumber to ensure the correct assembly type and placement, since an incorrectly installed assembly can fail its first required test.

What happens during the actual test itself?

A certified tester attaches gauges to the assembly's test cocks and checks each check valve and relief valve under controlled pressure conditions, typically taking 20 to 30 minutes per assembly.
