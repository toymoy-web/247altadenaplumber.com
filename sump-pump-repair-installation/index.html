A sump pump that fails during a storm can mean standing water in a crawlspace or low-lying part of the property within hours. That risk is higher than average in parts of Altadena right now, since burned hillside soil in the Eaton Fire area sheds rainfall instead of absorbing it, which has triggered real debris flow and flash flood warnings for the burn scar during multiple storms since the fire.

Why This Matters More In Altadena Right Now

The National Weather Service and LA County Public Works have issued evacuation warnings for parts of the Eaton Fire burn scar during several storms since the fire, including as recently as February 2026, specifically because burned slopes no longer absorb rain the way vegetated hillsides do. Water that would normally soak into the ground instead runs off quickly toward lower-lying streets and properties.

A working sump pump with a battery backup is one of the few practical defenses a homeowner has against that runoff reaching a crawlspace or low point on the property, particularly during a storm when grid power is more likely to go out at the exact moment the pump is needed most.

Signs Your Sump Pump Needs Attention

Pump Doesn't Turn On

A float switch stuck in the down position, a tripped breaker, or a seized motor can all keep a pump from activating when water rises.

Running Constantly

A pump that never shuts off may have a stuck float switch or be undersized for the amount of water actually reaching the pit.

Loud Grinding Or Rattling

Worn bearings or debris caught in the impeller often show up as noise well before the pump fails completely.

Visible Rust Or Old Age

Most sump pumps last 7 to 10 years. A unit already in that range is worth testing before the next storm rather than waiting for a failure.

How We Diagnose It

We start by testing the float switch and check valve, two of the most common failure points, before assuming the motor itself has failed. A pump that runs but doesn't move water is often a stuck or backward check valve rather than a dead motor.

For a battery backup system, we test the battery's actual charge capacity and the backup pump's independent operation, since a backup system that has never been tested under load can fail at the exact moment the primary pump loses power.

Any new installation or replacement follows Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements for discharge line routing and backflow prevention at the point where the line connects to the home's drainage system.

Repair Or Replace

Usually Repairable

A stuck float switch, a clogged intake screen, or a failed check valve on a pump under 5 years old.

Usually Replace

A motor that has seized, a pump already past 7 to 10 years old, or one that was undersized for the property from the start.

Worth Adding

A battery backup system for any home relying on a single pump with no backup power source, especially given how often storms here also bring power outages.

What Affects The Cost Of Sump Pump Service

Pump Type

A pedestal pump costs less than a submersible unit, though submersible pumps are quieter and more common in finished spaces.

Backup System

Adding battery backup to an existing pump costs less than installing a full backup system alongside a new primary pump.

Discharge Line Access

A discharge line that already has a clear path to daylight or the storm drain system costs less to connect than one needing new routing.

Timing

Standard daytime scheduling costs less than an emergency call placed during an active storm.

Homeowner's insurance often covers sudden water damage from a pump failure during a covered event, but routine pump replacement due to age is typically treated as maintenance rather than a covered loss. Check your specific policy before assuming either way.

Common Questions

How long does a sump pump typically last?

Most sump pumps last 7 to 10 years with normal use. A unit already in or past that range is worth testing proactively rather than waiting for it to fail during a storm.

Do I need a battery backup if I already have a sump pump?

If your home relies on a single pump with no backup power source, a battery backup system is worth considering, especially since storms that strain the pump are also the ones most likely to knock out power.

Does the Eaton Fire burn scar actually increase my flood risk?

Burned hillside soil sheds rainfall instead of absorbing it, which is why LA County and the National Weather Service have issued real evacuation and flash flood warnings for parts of the Eaton Fire burn scar during multiple storms since the fire.

Can a sump pump be installed in a home that doesn't already have one?

Yes, as long as there's a suitable low point to install a pit and a viable path for the discharge line, which we can assess during an on-site visit.

What's the difference between a pedestal and a submersible sump pump?

A pedestal pump sits above the pit with the motor out of the water, while a submersible pump sits inside the pit itself. Submersible units run quieter and are more common in finished spaces.

Is emergency sump pump service available during a storm?

Yes. Our emergency line is answered 24 hours a day, every day, including during active storms when sump pump calls are most common.

Before We Arrive

  • If the pit is actively overflowing, a wet/dry shop vacuum can buy time by removing standing water while you wait, as long as it's safe to access the area.
  • Note whether the pump runs at all when you lift the float switch by hand. That single test tells us a lot about whether the problem is electrical or mechanical before we arrive.
  • If you have a battery backup system, check whether its indicator light is on. A dead or disconnected backup battery is one of the most common reasons a backup system doesn't engage during an outage.
