The January 7, 2025 Eaton Fire destroyed 9,413 structures across Altadena and the surrounding foothills. More than a year and a half later, most of the rebuild is still ahead of us, and plumbing rough-in is one of the pieces every rebuild needs before the walls close up.

Where Altadena's Rebuild Actually Stands

As of December 2025, only 23 of the nearly 6,000 residential properties with significant fire damage had completed all rebuilding and repairs. Just 43% of owners of those properties had even applied for or received permits. Most of the burn zone has not broken ground yet, which means the plumbing rough-in phase is still ahead of the large majority of Altadena rebuilds.

Every rebuild now falls under California's updated Wildland-Urban Interface Code (Title 24, Part 7), which took effect January 1, 2026, and sets higher standards for fire-resistant construction, including fire sprinkler requirements on many rebuilt homes. A sprinkler system changes the water supply and backflow planning for a house in ways a pre-fire home never had to account for.

Where Plumbing Fits Into The Rebuild Timeline

Phase 1: Planning Approval

LA County Regional Planning reviews zoning, setbacks, and site plan, typically within 9 to 14 business days for a like-for-like rebuild.

Phase 2: Building & Safety Review

Full construction documents go through structural and code review, with the Ready to Issue (RTI) permit following 4 to 8 weeks later depending on correction rounds.

Rough-In Window

Once framing starts, plumbing rough-in (supply, drain, waste, vent, and gas lines) needs to be scheduled and inspected before insulation and drywall close the walls.

Final Fixture Install

Water heater, fixtures, and final connections go in near the end of the build, ahead of final inspection and occupancy.

What Rebuild Plumbing Actually Covers

A full rebuild needs a new water service line from the meter, complete interior supply and drain rough-in, gas line installation sized for every planned appliance, and a new water heater or tankless unit, all built to current Uniform Plumbing Code requirements rather than matched to whatever was there before the fire.

Like-for-like rebuild projects in the Eaton Fire area may increase floor area by up to 200 square feet or 10 percent, whichever is greater, which is worth factoring into fixture count and pipe sizing even on a project that is otherwise rebuilding the same footprint.

Coordinating Around The County Process

All permits run through LA County's electronic EPIC-LA portal, and we coordinate our rough-in scheduling against your permit's actual status there.

Contractor Schedule Coordination

Common Questions

Do you work directly with my rebuild contractor?

Yes. We coordinate rough-in scheduling directly with your general contractor around the framing and inspection timeline rather than working independently of the build.

Does the new fire code affect my plumbing plans?

It can. The Wildland-Urban Interface Code that took effect January 1, 2026 requires fire sprinkler systems on many rebuilt homes, which affects water supply sizing and backflow prevention planning.

Can I increase my home's size during a like-for-like rebuild?

In the Eaton Fire area, like-for-like projects may increase floor area by up to 200 square feet or 10 percent, whichever is greater, which we account for when planning fixture count and pipe sizing.

Are permit fees waived for fire rebuild homeowners?

LA County waives certain fees for qualifying owners who both owned and lived in the property before January 7, 2025. Confirm your specific eligibility with the county's permit center.

What To Have Ready Before We Start Rough-In

  • Have your approved Phase 2 construction documents available, since rough-in has to match the reviewed plan set exactly for inspection to pass.
  • Confirm your framing timeline with your general contractor so rough-in scheduling lines up with when walls are open and accessible.
  • If your rebuild includes a fire sprinkler system, confirm that scope with your contractor early, since it affects the water supply sizing we plan for.
