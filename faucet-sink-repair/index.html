A dripping faucet wastes more water than most people realize, and it is usually one worn part rather than a sign the whole fixture needs replacing. A slow kitchen or bathroom sink is often a separate, simpler fix.

Why Faucets Wear Out Faster In Altadena

Hard water across Altadena's mutual water company service areas leaves mineral scale inside faucet cartridges and aerators. That buildup is a common reason a faucet starts dripping or loses pressure years before it should.

Any replacement fixture installed in Altadena has to meet California's lead-free requirement under NSF/ANSI 372, part of the state's AB 1953 framework for fixtures carrying water for human consumption. We only install compliant parts.

Older homes with original fixtures from a prior remodel sometimes have supply line connections or valve types that are no longer common, which is worth checking before assuming a modern replacement faucet will connect without an adapter.

Common Faucet And Sink Problems

Persistent Dripping

Almost always a worn cartridge or valve seat, not a sign the entire faucet body has failed.

Low Flow At One Fixture

A clogged aerator from mineral buildup is the most common cause of reduced flow at a single faucet.

Leak Under The Sink

Usually a loose or corroded supply line connection, distinct from the faucet itself.

Handle Hard To Turn

Mineral buildup inside the cartridge can make a handle stiff or cause it to stick in one position.

How We Diagnose It

Most dripping faucets are diagnosed by pulling the handle and inspecting the cartridge or valve seat directly for wear or mineral scale, rather than replacing the whole fixture on assumption.

A leak under the sink gets traced to its exact connection point, whether that is the supply line, the shutoff valve, or the drain trap, since each has a different repair.

For a kitchen sink with both hot and cold sides affected, we also check the shared shutoff valves themselves, since a valve that no longer seals fully can mimic a faucet leak even when the faucet itself is fine.

Repair Or Replace

Usually Repairable

A dripping single-handle faucet with a worn cartridge, or a clogged aerator restricting flow.

Usually Replace

A faucet with a cracked body, a discontinued cartridge that is no longer available, or persistent leaks after repeated repairs.

Supply Line Fix

A corroded or loose supply line connection under the sink is a separate, usually inexpensive repair from the faucet itself.

Common Questions

Why does my faucet still drip after I replaced the washer myself?

Modern faucets typically use a cartridge rather than a rubber washer. If the cartridge itself is worn or scaled, replacing a washer will not fix the drip.

Can hard water really damage a faucet?

Yes. Mineral scale builds up inside cartridges and aerators over time, which is a leading cause of dripping and reduced flow in this area.

Do new faucets have to meet a specific standard in California?

Yes. Any fixture carrying water for human consumption must meet the NSF/ANSI 372 lead-free standard under California's AB 1953 framework, and we only install compliant fixtures.

How long does a typical faucet repair take?

Most cartridge or aerator repairs are completed in under an hour once the fixture is diagnosed, though a full faucet replacement can take longer depending on the connections underneath.
