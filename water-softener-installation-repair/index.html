A water softener removes the calcium and magnesium minerals responsible for hard water, which protects fixtures, appliances, and water heaters from the scale buildup common across Altadena's water supply.

Why Hard Water Is A Real Issue In Altadena

Altadena's water comes from a mix of mutual water companies, each drawing from somewhat different sources, so hardness levels can vary by which company serves your specific block. Most of the area falls in the hard to very hard range on standard water hardness scales.

That mineral content is the same cause behind the scale buildup we see repeatedly on water heaters, faucet cartridges, and toilet fill valves across the city, which is why a whole-home softener addresses several of those problems at their source.

Because Altadena has no single water provider, two households a short walk apart can have meaningfully different hardness readings, which is why we measure each property rather than sizing a system off a general area estimate.

Signs You Have A Hard Water Problem

Scale Buildup On Fixtures

White, crusty mineral deposits around faucets and showerheads are a direct sign of hard water.

Reduced Soap Lather

Hard water minerals bind with soap, reducing lather and often leaving a filmy residue on skin and dishes.

Premature Appliance Wear

Water heaters, dishwashers, and washing machines fail earlier than expected in hard water conditions.

Spotting On Dishes And Glassware

Mineral deposits drying on glassware and dishes even after washing is a common hard water complaint.

Sizing And Installing A Softener

We test your specific water hardness level rather than assuming a citywide average, since it can vary depending on which mutual water company serves your address.

Sizing considers household size, daily water usage, and the measured hardness level to select a system with the right resin capacity and regeneration cycle for your home.

New Installation Vs. Repair

New Installation

Sized to your home's water usage and measured hardness level, installed at the point where water enters the house.

Valve Or Control Head Repair

A softener that is not regenerating properly often needs a valve or control head repair rather than full replacement.

Resin Replacement

An older softener that is not softening water effectively may just need its resin bed replaced.

Common Questions

How do I know if my water is actually hard?

Visible scale on fixtures, reduced soap lather, and spotting on dishes are common signs, but we can measure your specific hardness level directly during a visit.

Will a softener fix the scale already in my water heater?

A softener prevents new scale buildup going forward, but existing buildup inside a water heater tank is not removed by adding a softener afterward.

How much maintenance does a water softener need?

Most systems need periodic salt refills and occasional resin bed checks, which we can walk you through at installation.

Will a softener reduce my water pressure?

A properly sized system should not cause a noticeable pressure drop. An undersized softener is a common cause of pressure complaints, which is why correct sizing matters.

Can a softener be installed alongside a whole-home filtration system?

Yes. Softening and filtration address different concerns and are commonly installed together, with the softener typically placed first in the water line.

Does a softener use a lot of water during regeneration?

Modern demand-initiated softeners regenerate only when needed based on actual usage, which uses significantly less water than older timer-based systems that regenerate on a fixed schedule.
