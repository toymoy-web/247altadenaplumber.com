Installing a new water heater is a different job than repairing an old one. It means sizing the right unit for the household, meeting current California code requirements, and, for rebuild projects, coordinating with a contractor's inspection schedule.

When Altadena Homeowners Need A New Installation

A tank water heater generally lasts 8 to 12 years before repeated repairs stop making financial sense, and replacement becomes the more reasonable path once a unit shows tank corrosion or repeated element failures.

New construction and Eaton Fire rebuild projects need a full new installation rather than a swap, including venting, gas or electrical connections, and the seismic strapping California requires on every water heater installation regardless of brand or capacity.

A household that has added bathrooms or people since the original unit was sized is another common reason for a planned upgrade, since an undersized tank runs out of hot water during typical morning or evening peak demand.

Signs It Is Time For A New Unit

Age Past 10-12 Years

Even a currently working tank this age is a reasonable candidate for planned replacement before it fails without warning.

Rust In The Water

Rust-colored hot water usually means the tank itself is corroding from the inside, which repair cannot reverse.

Rebuild Or Remodel

New construction or a fire rebuild needs a fresh installation sized and vented for the current code, not a like-for-like swap.

Rising Household Demand

A growing household outgrowing an undersized tank is a common reason to size up during a planned replacement rather than waiting for a failure.

Sizing And Installing The Right Unit

Sizing starts with household size and peak hot water demand, comparing a standard tank against a tankless unit's on-demand output for the home's actual usage pattern.

Every installation follows Uniform Plumbing Code requirements for venting clearance, temperature and pressure relief valve routing, and the seismic strapping California mandates in every jurisdiction, inspected at final sign-off on rebuild projects.

For a gas unit, we also confirm the existing gas line can support the new unit's BTU demand before installation day, since an undersized line is a common reason an installation gets delayed at inspection.

Tank Vs. Tankless

Tank

Lower upfront cost and simpler installation, with a fixed hot water supply based on tank size.

Tankless

Higher upfront cost but on-demand hot water and typically a longer service life than a tank unit.

Rebuild Projects

Either option works for a fire rebuild, and the choice usually comes down to available gas line capacity and space.

Common Questions

Should I replace my water heater before it fails completely?

If it is past 10-12 years old, planned replacement avoids an emergency failure and lets you choose the unit rather than reacting to a flood.

Do you install water heaters for Eaton Fire rebuild permits?

Yes. We install new water heaters as part of rebuild final inspections and coordinate timing with your contractor's schedule.

Is tankless worth the higher upfront cost?

For households with high or spread-out hot water demand, the on-demand supply and longer lifespan often justify it. For smaller households, a tank is often the more cost-effective choice.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater?

Yes, a permit and inspection are standard for water heater installation under LA County requirements, and we handle that as part of the installation.

Before We Arrive

  • Know your household's approximate peak hot water usage (how many bathrooms run close together) so we can size the replacement correctly on the first visit.
  • If this is for a rebuild, have your contractor's inspection schedule and any code requirements from your permit on hand.
  • Clear the area around the existing water heater so it is accessible for removal and the new unit's installation.
