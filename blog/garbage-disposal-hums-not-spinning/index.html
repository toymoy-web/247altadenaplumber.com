Why Does My Garbage Disposal Hum But Not Spin?

By the Altadena 24/7 Plumbing team

Talk To A Plumber
(626) 898-6169
Call Now

A garbage disposal that hums when switched on but does not spin is one of the most common kitchen calls we get from Altadena and Pasadena homes alike, and the good news is that it is usually a jam rather than a failed motor.

What The Humming Actually Means

The hum means the motor is receiving power and trying to turn, but something is physically blocking the impeller blades from rotating. This is different from a disposal that makes no sound at all, which points to a power issue instead.

How To Clear A Jam Safely

First, switch off the disposal and unplug it or shut off its breaker. Never put your hand inside a disposal, even when you believe it is off.

Most disposals have a hex-shaped opening on the bottom of the unit. Inserting an offset hex wrench there and turning it back and forth can free a jam from outside the unit without ever reaching inside.

If you do not have the hex wrench, tongs or needle-nose pliers reaching down from the top, with the power still off, can sometimes remove the obstruction directly.

After Clearing The Jam

Most disposals have a small red reset button on the bottom of the unit. Pressing it after clearing a jam is often necessary before the unit will run again, since the motor trips a safety switch when it senses resistance.

What Usually Causes The Jam

Fibrous vegetable scraps like celery or corn husks, grease, coffee grounds, and bones are the most common culprits. None of these break down well in a disposal, even one rated to handle food waste generally.

If Clearing The Jam Does Not Fix It

If the disposal still hums without spinning after you have cleared any visible obstruction, the motor bearing itself may have failed, which is typically a sign to replace rather than repair, especially on a unit already several years old.

Why This Is Such A Common Altadena Kitchen Call

Fibrous food waste and grease are the most common causes of a jam regardless of location, but hard water mineral buildup on internal disposal components adds to wear over time across Altadena's water service areas specifically, which shortens how long a unit lasts before motor issues start showing up.

Many disposals in Altadena's older kitchens are original to a prior remodel and are now well past a typical 8 to 12 year service life, which is often the underlying reason a unit finally stops resetting after a jam rather than the specific obstruction being unusually severe.

When It Is Time To Replace Rather Than Repair

If the disposal still hums without spinning after you have cleared any visible obstruction, the motor bearing itself may have failed, which is typically a sign to replace rather than repair, especially on a unit already several years old.

A leak from the bottom of the unit, separate from the humming issue, usually means a worn internal seal, which on most residential disposals is not cost-effective to repair given how close the labor cost runs to a new unit.

When replacement is the right call, it is also a reasonable time to reconsider horsepower and capacity, since a household's cooking habits and food waste volume may have changed since the original unit was installed.

Common Questions

Is it safe to use an Allen wrench I already have instead of the disposal's hex wrench?

Most disposals use a standard 1/4-inch hex size, so a wrench from a general set often works, but check your specific model's manual to confirm the size before forcing anything.

How often do garbage disposals typically need replacement?

Most residential disposals last 8 to 12 years with normal use, similar to a water heater's typical lifespan.

Can running cold water while using the disposal prevent jams?

Yes, cold water helps solidify grease so it gets chopped and flushed through rather than coating the blades and housing, which reduces jam frequency over time.

What size disposal do I actually need?

Household size and typical food waste volume determine whether a standard or higher horsepower unit makes sense, which is worth discussing before simply replacing like for like.

Can ice cubes really sharpen disposal blades?

Disposal blades are actually impellers, not sharp blades, so ice does not sharpen anything, though running ice through occasionally can help dislodge minor buildup.

Is it normal for a disposal to smell bad even when it is working fine?

A persistent odor usually means trapped food debris in the splash guard or lower chamber, which a thorough cleaning resolves, rather than a mechanical problem with the unit itself.

The Bottom Line

A humming, non-spinning disposal is one of the more fixable kitchen problems, and clearing the jam yourself is safe as long as the power is off first. If the hum continues after clearing any obstruction, that is the point to call for a professional diagnosis rather than continuing to try to force it, especially on an older unit where the motor itself may be the real issue. Trying to force a jammed disposal repeatedly risks damaging the motor further, which turns a simple fix into a full replacement.

Safety Steps Before You Clear A Jam

  • Switch off the disposal at the wall switch, then unplug it or shut off its breaker before doing anything else.
  • Do not put your hand inside the disposal at any point, even when you believe the power is off.
  • Use an offset hex wrench in the bottom opening, or tongs from the top, to clear the obstruction from outside the unit.
