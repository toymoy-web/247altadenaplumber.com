Why Do Drains Keep Clogging in Older Homes?

By the Altadena 24/7 Plumbing team

Talk To A Plumber
(626) 898-6169
Call Now

If a drain clears easily after snaking but clogs again within a few weeks, the recurring pattern usually points to something about the pipe itself, not just what went down it that particular week.

Root Intrusion In Aging Laterals

Many homes built before 1970 still run on original clay or cast iron sewer laterals. Both materials are vulnerable to root intrusion at pipe joints, and mature trees near the line's path, common on older, tree-lined streets, are a leading cause of a lateral that clogs again and again in the same general area.

A snake can punch a path through root intrusion, but it does not remove the roots themselves, which continue growing back toward the same moisture and nutrient source inside the pipe.

Grease And Scale Buildup

Grease poured down a kitchen drain, even in small amounts over years, coats the inside of the pipe and narrows its effective diameter gradually. A snake clears a channel through that buildup without removing the coating along the rest of the pipe wall.

Mineral scale from hard water adds to this narrowing over time in a way that is separate from grease but has a similar cumulative effect on flow.

Bellied Or Settled Pipe Sections

Ground settling over decades can create a low spot, or belly, in an underground pipe where water and debris collect instead of flowing through. This is a structural issue that snaking cannot fix, since the problem is the pipe's shape, not a blockage inside it.

How To Actually Fix A Recurring Clog

A sewer camera inspection shows which of these causes is actually happening in your specific line, rather than guessing based on symptoms alone.

Hydro jetting is the right fix for grease and scale buildup, since it scours the full pipe diameter rather than just punching through the middle. Root intrusion from a cracked joint usually needs the underlying pipe damage addressed, whether through trenchless lining or replacement, or the roots will keep coming back.

Common Questions

Can I use chemical drain cleaner on a recurring clog?

We would not recommend it repeatedly. Chemical cleaners can damage older pipe over time and do not address root intrusion or a structural issue, which is the actual cause behind most recurring clogs.

How do I know if a clog is grease-related or root-related?

A camera inspection distinguishes between the two clearly. Grease buildup looks like a coating along the pipe wall, while root intrusion appears as visible root mass entering through a joint or crack.

Will hydro jetting damage an older, more fragile pipe?

We assess pipe condition with a camera first. Jetting is safe for sound pipe but is not the right tool for a line already compromised by heavy corrosion or cracking.

Does removing a tree near my sewer line fix a root problem?

Removing the tree stops new root growth from that source, but existing roots already inside the pipe need to be cleared separately, and the underlying crack or joint issue they entered through still needs repair.

How often should I have my drains professionally cleaned as prevention?

For a home with a history of recurring clogs, an annual or biannual preventative cleaning is reasonable. A home with no history of clogs does not necessarily need a fixed schedule.

Can a garbage disposal contribute to kitchen drain clogs over time?

Yes, especially if fibrous scraps or grease are ground up and sent into a line already narrowed by scale, which is why disposal habits matter for the drain line beyond the disposal itself.

The Bottom Line

A drain that keeps clogging is telling you something about the pipe, not just reacting to whatever went down it that week. A camera inspection turns that guessing game into an actual diagnosis, which is the only way to pick the fix that actually lasts instead of paying for another snaking visit in a few weeks.
