5 Warning Signs You May Have a Slab Leak

By the Altadena 24/7 Plumbing team

Call Now

A slab leak develops in a water line running underneath your home's concrete foundation, and because it is hidden, most homeowners do not know they have one until it has already been running for weeks or months. These five signs are the ones we see most often before a slab leak gets diagnosed.

1. A Warm Patch On Your Floor

If a section of tile or concrete flooring feels noticeably warmer than the rest of the room, especially without a radiant heating system in the house, that warmth usually means a hot water line is leaking directly beneath it.

The warm spot will not always be large. Sometimes it is a single tile near a wall or hallway that just feels off compared to the flooring around it.

2. Your Water Bill Jumps For No Clear Reason

A slab leak runs continuously, day and night, whether anyone is home or not. That steady water loss shows up as a bill that climbs without any change in household habits.

If your bill jumped and you cannot point to a reason, like a new appliance or guests staying longer than usual, it is worth checking your water meter next.

3. Your Water Meter Keeps Moving With Everything Off

Turn off every fixture in the house, then check your water meter. If the dial is still moving, water is leaving the system somewhere, and a slab leak is one of the more serious possibilities.

This test only tells you that a leak exists somewhere in the system. It does not confirm the leak is under the slab specifically, which is why professional acoustic detection is the next step rather than guessing.

4. You Hear Running Water With No Fixture On

A pressurized line leaking under a slab can sometimes be faintly audible, especially in a quiet house at night. If you hear water movement and cannot trace it to any fixture, do not assume it will resolve on its own.

5. Cracking Or Damage In Flooring Or Baseboards

Moisture pushing up through a slab over time can cause tile to crack or baseboards to warp along the path of the leak. By the time this sign appears, the leak has usually been active for a while.

What To Do If You Notice These Signs

None of these signs alone confirms a slab leak, but two or more together is worth a professional diagnosis before you assume the cause. Acoustic detection and pressure isolation can confirm the leak's location without cutting into concrete on a guess.

Altadena's mix of clay, sandy loam, and adobe soils shifts with seasonal moisture, and that movement is a real contributor to slab leaks in this area, particularly in homes built between the 1950s and 1980s on copper supply lines under the slab.

Slab Leaks We See Most Often In Altadena

Homes built between the 1950s and 1980s across Altadena commonly run copper supply lines directly under a concrete slab foundation. Copper has a real service life, and once a pinhole opens from age or a reaction with the surrounding soil, water travels along the underside of the slab before it ever surfaces where you can see it.

The clay, sandy loam, and adobe soil mix common across the San Gabriel foothills adds mechanical stress on top of that aging process, since seasonal moisture changes shift the ground enough to flex a rigid copper line resting against concrete until a small crack becomes an active leak.

How Slab Leak Repair Actually Works

Once acoustic detection narrows the leak to a specific area, we isolate sections of the home's plumbing and pressure test each one to confirm exactly which line is affected, hot or cold side, before recommending a repair method.

From there, the choice usually comes down to three options: a spot repair through a small opening in the slab directly over the leak, a reroute that moves the line through a wall or attic space instead of under the floor, or in the case of multiple leaks, a full repipe of the affected line. Each has a different cost and disruption profile, which is why the diagnosis matters as much as the repair itself.

Whichever method applies, the repair uses materials and methods meeting Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standards, the same code every plumbing repair in California is held to.

Common Questions

How much does slab leak repair typically cost?

It depends heavily on the repair method. A spot repair through the slab, a reroute above the slab, or a full repipe of the affected line each carry a different scope, which is why we quote after acoustic detection confirms the leak's exact location.

Can a slab leak go away on its own?

No. A slab leak does not self-repair, and the longer it runs, the more water damage and soil erosion under the foundation it can cause.

Is a slab leak covered by homeowners insurance?

Many policies cover the resulting damage, though coverage for locating and accessing the leak itself varies by policy. Check your specific coverage before assuming either way.

Can I keep living in my home during slab leak repair?

In almost all cases, yes. Access work is typically limited to one room or a small section of flooring, and water service to the rest of the house is not usually interrupted for long.

Does a slab leak mean my whole foundation is at risk?

Not typically if it is caught reasonably early. Extended, unaddressed leaks over many months can affect soil support under the slab, which is part of why early diagnosis matters.

The Bottom Line

A slab leak is one of the few plumbing problems where waiting genuinely makes things worse every week it continues. If you recognize two or more of the signs above, scheduling acoustic detection sooner rather than later keeps the eventual repair smaller and less disruptive than letting it run for months. The diagnosis is quick, non-invasive, and it is the only reliable way to know which repair approach actually fits your situation instead of guessing and cutting concrete in the wrong spot.
