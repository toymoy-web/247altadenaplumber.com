Signs Your Water Heater Is About to Fail

By the Altadena 24/7 Plumbing team

Talk To A Plumber
(626) 898-6169
Call Now

Most water heaters do not fail without warning. There is usually a stretch of days or weeks where the unit shows a sign that something is wrong before it stops working completely, or worse, starts leaking. Here is what to watch for.

Rumbling Or Popping Sounds During Heating

Sediment settles at the bottom of a tank over years, especially in a hard-water area. As the burner or element heats the water, steam pockets form under that hardened sediment layer and escape with a knocking or rumbling sound.

This sign alone does not mean the tank is failing, but it does mean the unit is working harder than it should to reach the same temperature, which shortens its remaining service life.

Rusty Or Discolored Hot Water

Rust-colored water from the hot side only, not the cold, usually points to internal tank corrosion or a failing anode rod. Once the tank itself is corroding, repair options narrow quickly.

Water Pooling Around The Base

Moisture at the bottom of a water heater can come from a loose fitting, a failed pressure relief valve, or a cracked tank. The first two are repairable. A cracked tank is not, and it means replacement.

Inconsistent Or Insufficient Hot Water

A tank that used to last through a full shower but now runs cold halfway through often has a failing heating element (on electric units) or a sediment-insulated burner (on gas units) that cannot heat the full tank volume anymore.

The Unit Is Approaching 10-12 Years Old

Age alone is a signal worth planning around. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, and a unit near or past that range is a reasonable candidate for planned replacement, even if it is still technically working, rather than waiting for a failure that floods a closet or garage.

Why This Matters More In Altadena

Homes here are served by a mix of small mutual water companies, and hardness varies block to block depending on which one delivers your water. That mineral content is a documented cause of early element and burner failure, which is why a water heater in Altadena often does not make it to the upper end of its expected service life without some sediment-related symptom first.

Why Altadena Water Heaters Fail Earlier Than Average

Altadena does not have one single water provider. Homes here are served by a mix of small mutual water companies, and hardness varies block to block depending on which one delivers your water. That mineral content settles at the bottom of a tank over years, insulating the burner or heating element from the water above it.

The unit has to work harder to reach the same temperature as sediment builds, and that extra strain is a documented, common reason a water heater in this area fails years before its rated lifespan compared to the same unit running on softer water.

What A Professional Diagnosis Actually Checks

On a gas unit, a technician checks the thermocouple and gas pressure with a manometer before assuming the whole burner assembly needs replacing. On an electric unit, a multimeter tests each heating element and thermostat separately, since only one may have failed even though both showed symptoms.

The anode rod also gets pulled and inspected on any tank over five years old. A rod that has worn down to bare wire is no longer protecting the tank from corrosion, and replacing it can meaningfully extend a tank's remaining service life for a relatively small cost.

Any replacement is installed to Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements for venting, relief valve routing, and the seismic strapping California requires on every water heater regardless of brand.

Common Questions

Should I repair or replace a failing water heater?

A unit under 8 years old with a single failed part, like a thermocouple or heating element, is usually worth repairing. A unit past 10-12 years, or one showing tank corrosion, is generally a better candidate for replacement.

How long does a water heater replacement take?

Most straightforward tank replacements are completed in a few hours. A switch to tankless, or a unit requiring new venting, can take longer depending on what has to change.

Can I flush my own water heater to remove sediment?

Some homeowners do this themselves, though a professional flush also includes inspecting the anode rod and checking for other early warning signs while the tank is already being serviced.

Why does my water heater make noise only sometimes?

Intermittent rumbling often correlates with how much sediment has settled since the last flush and how hard the unit is working during a given heating cycle, so it can vary day to day.

Is a leaking pressure relief valve dangerous?

A properly functioning relief valve occasionally releasing a small amount of pressure is normal. A valve leaking continuously needs replacement, since it is a safety component, not just a maintenance item.

The Bottom Line

None of these signs by themselves mean your water heater is about to fail tomorrow, but together they tell a story about a unit nearing the end of its service life. Catching that story early means choosing a replacement on your own schedule instead of reacting to a flooded closet or garage, and it gives you time to compare tank and tankless options rather than grabbing whatever is available during an emergency.

Before You Call For A Diagnosis

  • Note whether the issue is noise, discoloration, a leak, or reduced hot water, since each points to a different likely cause.
  • If you see water pooling at the base, shut off the unit's water supply and, on an electric unit, its breaker as a precaution.
  • Check the unit's approximate age on its rating plate, since that alone affects whether repair or replacement makes more sense.
