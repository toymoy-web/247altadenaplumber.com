Hard Water 101: Signs You Have It and What to Do

By the Altadena 24/7 Plumbing team

Talk To A Plumber
(626) 898-6169
Call Now

Hard water contains high levels of calcium and magnesium, and across Altadena's mix of mutual water companies, hardness levels vary block to block but generally run hard to very hard. Here is how to tell if it is affecting your home and what actually fixes it.

The Visible Signs

White or chalky scale buildup around faucets, showerheads, and drains is the most obvious sign. That mineral deposit forms as hard water evaporates and leaves calcium and magnesium behind.

Spotting on dishes and glassware, even fresh out of the dishwasher, is another common complaint, along with a filmy residue on skin after showering, since hard water minerals bind with soap and reduce lather.

The Less Visible Damage

Mineral scale accumulates inside a water heater tank, faucet cartridges, and toilet fill valves, which is a documented cause of earlier appliance failure across all three. A water heater in a hard water area often needs replacement years sooner than the same unit would in a soft water area.

Scale inside pipes can also gradually narrow the interior diameter over many years, contributing to reduced water pressure in older plumbing systems.

How To Confirm It

Visible scale and the other signs above are a reasonable indicator, but measuring your actual water hardness gives you a specific number to work from rather than a guess, since hardness varies depending on which mutual water company serves your specific address.

What Actually Fixes It

A whole-home water softener is the direct fix for hardness specifically, using ion exchange to remove calcium and magnesium before water reaches your fixtures and appliances.

Softening is a different fix than filtration, which addresses taste, odor, chlorine, and sediment rather than mineral hardness. A household with both concerns can run a softener and a filtration system together, each solving its own problem.

Why Hardness Varies So Much Across Altadena

Altadena draws water from several distinct mutual water companies, including Lincoln Avenue Water Company in the western part of town and Rubio Canyon Land & Water Association, each pulling from somewhat different sources. That is the real reason two homes a few blocks apart can report noticeably different hardness levels and different scale-related complaints.

Knowing which company serves your specific address is a useful first step before assuming a citywide average applies, since a softener sized for one part of town's typical hardness may be undersized or oversized somewhere else nearby.

How A Softener Actually Solves This

A whole-home softener uses ion exchange resin to remove calcium and magnesium from water before it reaches any fixture or appliance in the house, addressing hardness at its source rather than treating symptoms individually.

Sizing considers household size, daily water usage, and your measured hardness level, since installing an undersized system for your actual usage means it will not keep up, while an oversized one is an unnecessary expense.

The system installs at the point where the main water line enters the home, so every fixture and appliance downstream benefits, rather than treating water individually at each tap.

Any softener installation follows Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements for bypass valve placement and backflow protection at the connection point.

Common Questions

How is water hardness actually measured?

Hardness is measured in grains per gallon (GPG) or parts per million (PPM) of dissolved calcium and magnesium. Most water testing services or a professional visit can give you an exact number for your address.

Does boiling water remove hardness?

Boiling can reduce temporary hardness slightly but does not meaningfully soften water for household use. A water softener is the practical fix for ongoing hardness.

How long does a water softener installation take?

Most residential softener installations are completed in a few hours, typically at the point where the main water line enters the home.

Will a softener make my water taste different?

Some people notice a slightly different taste or a mild slippery feel initially, both normal and generally something people adjust to quickly.

Does every home in the same neighborhood have the same hardness level?

Not necessarily, especially somewhere like Altadena, where hardness varies depending on which mutual water company serves your specific address.

Can hard water affect my skin and hair, not just my plumbing?

Many people notice drier skin and hair after showering in hard water, since the minerals interfere with soap and shampoo rinsing cleanly, leaving a residue behind on both.

How long does a water softener system typically last?

Most residential softener systems last 10 to 15 years with normal maintenance, though the resin bed itself may need replacement partway through that span depending on usage and hardness level.

The Bottom Line

Hard water is easy to live with day to day and expensive to ignore long term, since the real cost shows up in shortened appliance lifespans rather than anything immediately obvious. A softener sized to your actual measured hardness pays for itself in avoided repairs over time, and it is worth measuring your specific level rather than assuming a citywide average applies to your address. If you are already noticing scale on fixtures or replacing a water heater sooner than expected, measuring your hardness before deciding on a system gives you a number to plan around instead of guessing at the right size.
