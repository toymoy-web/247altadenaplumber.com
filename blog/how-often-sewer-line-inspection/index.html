How Often Should You Get Your Sewer Line Inspected?

By the Altadena 24/7 Plumbing team

Call Now

A sewer camera inspection shows the actual condition of your lateral line, root intrusion, cracks, offset joints, without any digging. The question is how often it is worth doing one before a problem actually shows up.

When It Is Worth Scheduling Proactively

A home with mature trees near the sewer line's path, especially older species with aggressive root systems, is a reasonable candidate for inspection every few years even without symptoms, since root intrusion into an aging clay or cast iron joint develops gradually before it causes a full backup.

A home built before 1970, which commonly means original clay or cast iron piping, is worth a baseline inspection even if nothing seems wrong yet, simply to know the line's actual condition before buying, selling, or doing a major remodel.

Signs That Mean You Should Not Wait

Recurring clogs that come back within weeks of snaking are the clearest sign something structural, not just debris, is going on. A snake clears a path through a blockage but does not fix a cracked pipe or root mass causing it.

A sewage smell in the yard, gurgling drains, or a soggy patch of lawn near the line's path are all reasons to schedule an inspection rather than waiting for a full backup.

What A Camera Inspection Actually Shows

The camera reveals exactly where a crack, offset joint, or root mass sits in the line and how much of the pipe is affected, which determines whether a spot repair, trenchless lining, or full replacement is the right fix.

Without a camera, choosing a repair method is a guess. With one, the repair matches the pipe's actual condition instead.

A Practical Schedule

For a home with no symptoms and modern PVC piping, inspection every 5 to 7 years is reasonable as a baseline check. For an older home with clay or cast iron piping and mature trees nearby, every 2 to 3 years is more appropriate, since the failure risk is meaningfully higher on that combination of factors.

Altadena's Housing Stock Makes This More Relevant

A large share of Altadena homes predate 1970, which commonly means original clay or cast iron sewer laterals still in service today. Both materials are vulnerable to root intrusion at aging joints, and Altadena's mature trees, planted decades ago along many older streets, are exactly the kind of root system that causes this over time.

For a property in this situation, treating inspection as routine maintenance rather than something to consider only after a backup is a genuinely cost-saving habit, not just a cautious one.

What Happens If You Skip Inspection And Something Goes Wrong

A full sewer backup without any warning is disruptive and often more expensive to resolve than a problem caught during a routine inspection, simply because emergency service and potential water damage cleanup get added to the actual pipe repair cost.

A camera inspection ahead of time also gives you options. If a problem is found early, spot repair or trenchless lining are often still viable. A problem discovered only after a full collapse may require more extensive excavation regardless of what would have worked earlier.

There is also a scheduling advantage to catching a problem early: a planned repair fits around your calendar, while an emergency backup fits around nothing but the emergency itself, usually at the least convenient possible moment.

Common Questions

Does a sewer camera inspection damage my pipes?

No. The camera passes through an existing cleanout or access point and does not touch or alter the pipe in any way.

How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

Most residential inspections take 30 to 60 minutes, depending on the length of the line and how many access points are available.

Should I get an inspection before buying a home?

Yes, especially for a home built before 1970. A sewer inspection is not always included in a standard home inspection, and it can reveal a costly problem before you close on the property.

Can I watch the camera feed during my inspection?

Yes, most technicians can show you the live feed and explain what you are looking at in real time, which helps when deciding on a repair approach afterward.

Do I need a permit just for a camera inspection?

No. A permit is only needed if the inspection leads to actual repair or replacement work on the line itself.

Can I schedule an inspection at the same time as a routine drain cleaning?

Yes, and it is often efficient to do both in one visit, especially if the drain cleaning technician notices something during the job that warrants a closer look.

The Bottom Line

A sewer camera inspection is inexpensive relative to what it can prevent. Knowing your line's actual condition, rather than assuming it is fine until it backs up, puts you in control of the timeline and the repair method instead of reacting to an emergency that limits your options and adds cleanup costs on top of the repair itself. For a home with mature trees or original pre-1970 piping, that small upfront cost is one of the more reliably worthwhile plumbing expenses a homeowner can make.
