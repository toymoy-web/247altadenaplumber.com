Why Is My Water Bill Suddenly So High?

By the Altadena 24/7 Plumbing team

A water bill that climbs without a change in household habits is one of the most reliable signs of a leak, and it is worth taking seriously before it becomes a bigger repair. Here is how to think through the possible causes.

Start With Your Water Meter

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, then check your meter. If the dial or digital readout is still moving, water is leaving the system somewhere between the meter and your fixtures.

Wait at least an hour with everything off, then check again for a more reliable reading, since some meters move slowly enough that a quick glance can miss it.

Common Causes, From Simple To Serious

A running toilet is the most common and least expensive cause. A worn flapper can waste hundreds of gallons a day silently, since the sound is often too quiet to notice.

An irrigation system leak is another frequent cause, especially on a line that runs underground and is not visible from the surface.

A leak inside the home's supply lines, whether in a wall, under a slab, or along the service line from the meter, is the more serious possibility, and it is the one that needs professional detection rather than a DIY fix.

Checking Your Toilets First

Put a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank and wait 10 minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper is leaking and letting tank water bypass into the bowl continuously.

When It Is Not The Toilets

If your toilets check out and the meter is still moving, the leak is likely in a supply line rather than a fixture. Acoustic leak detection can trace the sound of pressurized water escaping a pipe, even through concrete or soil, which narrows the search before anything gets opened up.

A slab leak specifically often shows up alongside a warm spot on the floor if it is on the hot water side, which is a useful clue when deciding how urgently to schedule a diagnosis.

A Note On Altadena's Water Providers

Because Altadena does not have one single water utility, comparing notes with a neighbor on a different mutual water company's line will not always tell you much, since rates and billing cycles can differ. Your own meter reading is the more reliable starting point regardless of which company serves your address.

When A Bill Spike Means More Than Just A Leak

Most of the time, a rising bill traces back to a single clear cause: a running toilet, an irrigation leak, or a supply line problem. Occasionally the cause is a combination, such as a running toilet masking a smaller slab leak that would otherwise be more noticeable on its own.

This is part of why checking your toilets first, then re-testing the meter afterward, matters. If the meter still moves after fixing an obvious running toilet, that confirms a second, separate issue rather than assuming the toilet explained the entire increase.

How Professional Leak Detection Narrows It Down

Electronic leak detection starts with acoustic listening equipment that picks up the sound pressurized water makes escaping a pipe, even through concrete or soil. That narrows the search area significantly before anything gets opened up or excavated.

For a suspected slab leak specifically, that acoustic tracing gets combined with a pressure test on isolated sections of the line, and thermal imaging can flag a hot water leak by showing the warm plume it creates under flooring, which is faster than relying on a warm spot alone.

Any repair that follows uses materials meeting Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standards, whether the fix is a simple fixture repair or a full line replacement.

Common Questions

How long does an average leak take to show up on a bill?

It depends on your water provider's billing cycle, but many homeowners notice within one to two billing periods, especially with a continuously running leak like a slab leak or a running toilet.

Can a leak outside my house, like an irrigation line, cause a bill spike?

Yes. Any leak on your side of the water meter counts toward your bill, whether it is inside the house, in an irrigation line, or along your property's water service line.

What if my meter shows no movement but my bill is still high?

If the meter is not moving with everything off, the increase may reflect an actual change in household usage rather than a leak, which is worth reviewing before assuming a hidden problem.

Should I contact my water company or a plumber first?

Your water provider can confirm the meter reading and rule out a billing error, but only a plumber can trace and repair a leak on your side of the meter, so both steps are often worthwhile.

Can a leak on a single fixture really account for a big bill jump?

Yes. A continuously running toilet or an undetected slab leak can waste hundreds of gallons a day, which adds up to a substantial increase over a full billing cycle even from one source.

The Bottom Line

A water bill spike is one of the clearest, earliest signals that something is wrong, often before any visible sign of a leak appears. Checking your meter takes ten minutes and either rules out a leak or gives you the evidence to move forward with a proper diagnosis, which is a much better position than waiting for a visible puddle or a warm spot to confirm what your bill already told you.
