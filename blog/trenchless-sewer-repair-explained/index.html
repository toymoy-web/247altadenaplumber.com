Trenchless Sewer Repair: Is It Right for Your Home?

By the Altadena 24/7 Plumbing team

Call Now

Trenchless sewer repair lets a damaged pipe get relined or replaced without excavating a full trench along its length. It is not the right fit for every situation, but when it applies, it can meaningfully reduce the disruption to your yard and driveway.

How Trenchless Lining Works

A cured-in-place liner is installed inside the existing damaged pipe through an access point, usually a cleanout, then cured in place to form a new pipe-within-a-pipe. This avoids excavating along the pipe's whole path.

How Trenchless Replacement (Pipe Bursting) Works

Pipe bursting pulls a new pipe through the path of the old one, breaking the old pipe outward as it goes. It replaces the pipe entirely rather than lining the inside of the existing one, which matters for a pipe that is too structurally compromised for lining alone.

When Trenchless Methods Are A Good Fit

A pipe with isolated cracks, root intrusion, or offset joints, but no full collapse, is usually a good trenchless candidate, especially when the path runs under a driveway, patio, or mature landscaping you would rather not disturb.

When Trenchless Is Not The Right Fit

A fully collapsed section of pipe cannot be lined, since there is no longer an intact structure to line. That situation needs open trench excavation at least for the collapsed portion.

A line with severe bellies (low spots where the pipe has sunk) or significant diameter changes along its path can also be poor trenchless candidates, since both methods work best on a relatively consistent, intact pipe.

Why The Camera Inspection Comes First

Choosing a method without a camera inspection is a guess. The inspection shows exactly what condition the pipe is in and how much of it is affected, which is the only reliable way to know whether trenchless lining, pipe bursting, or open trench replacement actually fits your specific situation.

Why Trenchless Methods Fit Altadena's Older Lots Well

Many Altadena properties have mature landscaping and established hardscape that predates the current sewer lateral's condition, which makes avoiding a full trench genuinely valuable here, not just a nice-to-have convenience.

Combined with the area's pre-1970s clay and cast iron piping being common candidates for root intrusion, trenchless lining specifically comes up often as the right match between the pipe's real condition and a homeowner's interest in protecting established trees and landscaping.

Why The Camera Inspection Always Comes First

Choosing a repair method without a camera inspection is a guess. The inspection shows exactly what condition the pipe is in and how much of it is affected, which is the only reliable way to know whether lining, bursting, or open trench replacement actually fits a specific situation.

A pipe with severe bellies, low spots where it has settled and sunk, or significant diameter changes along its path can be poor candidates for either trenchless method, since both work best on a relatively consistent, structurally intact pipe rather than one with major shape irregularities.

The inspection also documents baseline condition for the rest of the line, which is useful even for sections not being repaired right now, since it gives you a reference point for any future decision about that same pipe.

Whatever method gets chosen, the new or relined pipe meets Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) standards for drain, waste, and vent materials.

Common Questions

How long does a trenchless lining job take?

Most residential trenchless lining jobs are completed in a single day once the camera inspection and access points are confirmed.

Is trenchless repair more expensive than traditional excavation?

It depends on the specific job. Trenchless often costs less overall once you factor in landscaping and driveway restoration that a full trench would otherwise require.

Does trenchless lining reduce the pipe's interior diameter?

Slightly, since the liner adds a thin layer inside the existing pipe, but the reduction is minor enough that it does not meaningfully affect flow in most residential applications.

Can trenchless methods be used on any pipe material?

Most common residential materials, including clay, cast iron, and PVC, are compatible with trenchless lining, though the camera inspection confirms suitability for your specific line.

How long does a trenchless liner actually last?

A properly installed cured-in-place liner is typically rated for 50 years or more, comparable to or exceeding the lifespan of the original pipe material when it was new.

Do I need to be home during a trenchless repair?

It is helpful but not always required, as long as we have clear access to the cleanout or access point and can reach you if a decision needs to be made mid-job.

The Bottom Line

Trenchless methods have become the default first choice for a lot of sewer repairs precisely because they solve the problem without tearing up a yard. The real decision point is always what the camera inspection actually shows, not a general preference for one method over another, which is why we never skip that step before recommending an approach. A repair chosen based on real evidence rather than assumption is far more likely to actually resolve the problem the first time.
