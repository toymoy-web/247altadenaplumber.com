What's Normal Water Pressure for a House?

By the Altadena 24/7 Plumbing team

Call Now

Most residential plumbing codes consider 40 to 80 psi (pounds per square inch) the normal working range for household water pressure, with somewhere around 50 to 60 psi being ideal for most fixtures and appliances.

Why Pressure Below 40 PSI Is A Problem

Below 40 psi, showers and faucets feel noticeably weak, and appliances like dishwashers and washing machines may not fill or perform correctly, since they are designed around a minimum expected pressure.

Low pressure across the whole house, rather than a single fixture, often points to a supply-side issue: a partially closed main shutoff valve, corrosion narrowing the inside of aging pipe, or in some cases a problem on the utility side of the meter.

Why Pressure Above 80 PSI Is Also A Problem

High pressure is easy to overlook because it does not feel like a problem in the moment. Fixtures spray forcefully and showers feel powerful, but sustained high pressure stresses pipe joints, fittings, and appliance supply lines well beyond what they are rated for.

A pressure-reducing valve (PRV) installed where the main line enters the house is the standard fix for a property with municipal pressure running consistently high.

How To Check Your Own Pressure

A simple pressure gauge that threads onto an outdoor spigot, available at most hardware stores, gives you an actual reading in under a minute. Test it with no other fixtures running for the most accurate baseline number.

Why Pressure Can Vary By Location In Altadena

Because Altadena is served by several different mutual water companies rather than one utility, and homes sit at different elevations across the foothill terrain, pressure genuinely does vary block to block in ways that are not always about a problem with your own plumbing.

Why Pressure Genuinely Varies Across Altadena

Altadena's foothill terrain means real elevation differences between properties, and because the city is served by several different mutual water companies rather than one utility, pressure at the source can differ meaningfully depending on which company and which part of the distribution system serves your specific address.

This means comparing your pressure reading to a neighbor served by a different mutual company is not always a useful comparison. Testing your own address is the only way to know what is actually normal for your specific property.

Fixing Pressure That Is Outside The Normal Range

For pressure that is too low across the whole house, the fix depends on the cause: opening a partially closed main shutoff valve, replacing corroded pipe that has narrowed internally, or in some cases addressing a problem on the utility side of the meter that requires coordination with your water provider.

For pressure that runs consistently high, installing a pressure-reducing valve (PRV) where the main line enters the house is the standard fix, protecting every fixture and appliance supply line downstream from ongoing stress they were never rated to handle.

Common Questions

Why does my pressure change throughout the day?

Municipal water demand fluctuates by time of day, and pressure can dip during peak usage hours, like early morning, when many households are showering at once.

Can a pressure-reducing valve fail over time?

Yes. A PRV can wear out after years of use, which sometimes shows up as a gradual return of high pressure symptoms after the valve was working correctly for years.

Does low water pressure always mean a leak?

Not always. It can also mean a partially closed valve, corrosion narrowing an old pipe, or a utility-side issue, which is why testing your actual pressure is more useful than guessing at the cause.

Is it normal for pressure to differ between floors of a two-story house?

Yes, to a degree. Upper floors typically see somewhat lower pressure than the ground floor due to elevation, though a large difference can indicate an additional issue worth checking.

Can a water softener or filter reduce my pressure?

A properly sized system should not cause a noticeable reduction. A system that is undersized or has a clogged filter cartridge can restrict flow enough to feel like a pressure drop.

Does the size of my main water line affect my pressure?

Yes. An undersized main line, common in some older homes, can restrict flow enough to create a pressure problem even when the water source itself is delivering adequate pressure.

Should I check my pressure after any nearby street or utility work?

It is a reasonable precaution. Nearby construction or repair work can occasionally disturb a service line or fitting in ways that affect pressure at your specific property.

The Bottom Line

Water pressure sits in the background until it becomes a problem, either too weak to notice or too strong to ignore the wear it causes. A five-minute check with an inexpensive gauge tells you which situation you are actually in, rather than relying on how a shower simply feels, and it points directly toward the right fix instead of a guess. Whether the fix is a pressure-reducing valve, a service line repair, or simply confirming your main shutoff is fully open, knowing the actual number is always a better starting point than assuming.
