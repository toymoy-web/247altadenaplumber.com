A water heater that stops producing hot water, or starts making noise, rarely fixes itself. In Altadena, hard mineral content in the local water supply speeds up the wear that eventually causes a failure, so a repair call here often involves more sediment buildup than the same job in a softer-water city.

Why Altadena Water Heaters Fail Early

Altadena does not have one single water provider. Homes here are served by a mix of small mutual water companies, including Lincoln Avenue Water Company in the western part of town and Rubio Canyon Land & Water Association, plus a slice served directly by Pasadena Water and Power. Hardness and mineral content vary block to block depending on which company delivers your water.

That mineral content settles at the bottom of a tank over years, insulating the burner or heating element from the water above it. The unit has to work harder to reach the same temperature, and the extra strain is a common reason a Altadena water heater fails years before its rated lifespan.

Signs Your Water Heater Needs A Technician

No Hot Water

A dead pilot light, a tripped electric element, or a failed thermocouple can all cut off hot water completely.

Rumbling Or Popping

Sediment hardening at the bottom of the tank causes steam pockets that knock and rumble during heating cycles.

Rusty Or Cloudy Water

Discolored hot water often points to a corroding tank interior or a failing anode rod.

Water Around The Base

Moisture at the bottom of the tank can mean a loose fitting, a failed pressure relief valve, or a cracked tank.

How We Diagnose It

On a gas unit, a technician checks the thermocouple and gas pressure with a manometer before assuming the whole burner assembly needs replacing. On an electric unit, a multimeter tests each heating element and thermostat separately, since only one may have failed.

The anode rod gets pulled and inspected on any tank over five years old. A rod that has worn down to bare wire is no longer protecting the tank from corrosion, and replacing the fifteen-dollar rod can add years to a tank that would otherwise need full replacement.

Any replacement or new installation follows Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) requirements for venting, temperature and pressure relief valve routing, and seismic strapping, which California requires on every water heater regardless of the unit's brand.

Repair Or Replace

Usually Repairable

A failed thermocouple, heating element, thermostat, or pressure relief valve on a tank under 8 years old.

Usually Replace

Visible rust in the water, a tank leaking from the body itself, or a unit already past 10-12 years.

Worth A Second Look

A tank that is short-cycling or noisy but still heating water fully may just need a flush and anode check.

Common Questions

How old is too old for a water heater?

Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years. Once a unit passes that range, a repair on one part is often followed by a second failure within a year or two.

Can hard water really shorten a water heater's life?

Yes. Mineral scale insulates the heating surface and forces the unit to run longer per cycle, which is a documented cause of early element and burner failure.

Do you install water heaters for Eaton Fire rebuilds?

Yes. We install new water heaters as part of rebuild final inspections and coordinate timing with your contractor's schedule.

Before We Arrive

  • If you can locate your home's main water shutoff, turning it off stops water flow to a leaking heater while you wait, especially if you see water pooling at the base.
  • Take a photo of the water heater's rating plate (the sticker on the side listing model, serial number, and age) before we arrive. It speeds up sourcing the right anode rod or element on the first visit.
  • If the pilot light will not stay lit on a gas unit, do not keep re-lighting it repeatedly. Repeated attempts without success usually point to a failed thermocouple, not user error.
