A full sewer line replacement is a different scope than a repair. It comes into play when the camera inspection shows damage extensive enough that repeated spot repairs no longer make sense.

When Altadena Homes Need Full Replacement, Not Repair

A lateral with multiple offset joints, widespread root intrusion, or a section that has collapsed is usually a better candidate for full replacement than another round of spot repairs. This is common on pre-1970s Altadena properties still running original clay pipe.

A home moving through an Eaton Fire rebuild sometimes needs a new lateral simply because the old one was never designed for a larger rebuilt structure's fixture count, not because the original line had failed.

A line serving a property that changed hands or was subdivided over the decades can also have connections or transitions between different pipe materials, which is worth confirming by camera before assuming a single uniform pipe runs the full length.

Signs You Need Full Replacement

Multiple Failure Points

A camera inspection showing more than one damaged section usually means the whole line has reached the end of its service life.

Repeated Repairs Failing

If a spot repair or lining job did not resolve the problem for long, the surrounding pipe is likely in similar condition.

Collapsed Section

A fully collapsed section of pipe cannot be relined and needs to be excavated and replaced.

Rebuild Or Major Remodel

A significant addition or rebuild can outgrow the capacity of the original lateral, requiring a new line sized for the current fixture count.

Trenched Vs. Trenchless Replacement

Trenchless replacement (pipe bursting) pulls a new pipe through the path of the old one, breaking the old pipe outward as it goes. It avoids a full open trench but needs reasonably accessible entry and exit points.

New pipe material follows Uniform Plumbing Code standards for drain, waste, and vent systems, typically PVC for a residential lateral, selected for the specific soil and load conditions of the property.

Traditional trenched replacement digs along the full path of the line. It is more disruptive to landscaping and hardscape but is sometimes the only option when the line's path has obstacles trenchless methods cannot work around.

Choosing A Method

Pipe Bursting (Trenchless)

Faster and less disruptive to yards and driveways when access points allow it.

Open Trench Replacement

Necessary when the line's path has obstacles, tight bends, or connections that trenchless methods cannot accommodate.

Permit And Inspection

Full replacement requires an LA County permit and inspection, which we handle as part of the project.

What Affects The Cost Of Sewer Line Replacement

Scope Of The Problem

A straightforward, isolated repair costs less than a job involving multiple fixtures or a harder-to-diagnose root cause.

Parts & Materials

Fixture quality, pipe material, and whether a part needs to be special-ordered all factor into the final price.

Access & Location

A repair behind a finished wall, under a slab, or in a hard-to-reach crawlspace takes longer than one with open access.

Timing

Standard daytime scheduling costs less than an after-hours or weekend emergency dispatch.

Homeowner's insurance often covers sudden, accidental damage like a burst pipe, but typically does not cover gradual wear, an aging part failing on its own, or deferred maintenance. Check your specific policy for what it actually covers before assuming either way.

Common Questions

How long does a full sewer line replacement take?

A trenchless job is often completed in one to two days. An open trench replacement typically takes longer depending on the line's length and any landscaping restoration needed.

Will replacement damage my yard or driveway?

Trenchless methods limit disruption to the entry and exit points. Open trench replacement disturbs the ground along the pipe's path, which we discuss with you before starting.

Is a permit required for sewer line replacement?

Yes. LA County requires a permit and inspection for sewer lateral replacement, which we handle as part of the project.

Can I choose trenchless replacement even for a long lateral?

Length alone does not rule out trenchless replacement. Access points, bends, and the condition of the surrounding soil matter more than distance in determining whether it is a good fit.

Does replacement affect my home's water service during the work?

Sewer line work does not affect incoming water supply, only drainage, so most homes can limit disruption to avoiding drain use during active work hours.
