Water damage from a burst pipe, an overflowing appliance, or a roof leak needs fast extraction and drying to limit structural damage and mold growth. This is a distinct service from repairing the plumbing component that caused the leak in the first place.

Common Sources Of Water Damage In Altadena Homes

A burst supply line, a failed water heater, or an overflowing washing machine can each release enough water to soak flooring, drywall, and insulation within a short window. The pre-1970s housing stock common in Altadena often means older subfloor materials that absorb water quickly.

Homes moving through the rebuild process after the Eaton Fire also see water-service and plumbing work tied to new construction, and a temporary line or fitting failure during that work can create the same kind of water damage as an older home's aging pipe.

Appliance-related water damage is also common, since a supply-line hose behind a washing machine or an ice-maker fitting under a refrigerator can fail without any warning and release water for hours before it is noticed.

When To Call For Water Damage Restoration

Standing Water

Any visible standing water on flooring needs extraction before it soaks further into subfloor or drywall.

Soft Or Warped Flooring

Flooring that feels soft, bubbled, or warped underfoot has already absorbed water and needs drying, not just surface cleanup.

Musty Odor

A musty smell after any water event is an early sign of moisture trapped where you cannot see it, often behind walls or under flooring.

Visible Mold Growth

Mold appearing within 24 to 48 hours of a water event means moisture has been present long enough for growth to start.

How We Assess The Damage

A moisture meter checks how far water has traveled into drywall, subfloor, and framing, which is not always visible from the surface. That reading determines how much material needs to be dried versus removed.

Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers are placed based on that moisture mapping, and we track drying progress with follow-up meter readings rather than guessing when a space is dry.

Our extraction and drying process follows the IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration, the published industry reference for how affected materials should be assessed, dried, and verified before a job is considered complete.

What Restoration Covers, And What It Does Not

Extraction And Drying

Standing water removal and structural drying using commercial equipment, tracked to completion.

Mold Prevention

Antimicrobial treatment on affected materials to limit mold growth once an area has been dried.

Not Included: The Plumbing Fix

Repairing the burst pipe, failed valve, or leaking appliance connection itself is a separate plumbing repair, not part of restoration.

Common Questions

Do you also fix the pipe that caused the water damage?

Restoration and the plumbing repair are separate services with separate scopes, though we can coordinate both so the cause gets fixed along with the cleanup.

How fast does mold start after water damage?

Mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours in the right conditions, which is why extraction and drying need to start quickly.

Is water damage restoration covered by insurance?

Many homeowner policies cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe or appliance failure. We recommend confirming your specific coverage with your insurer.

How long does drying usually take?

Most residential drying jobs take three to five days with equipment running continuously, though the exact timeline depends on how much material absorbed water and how it is tracked with follow-up moisture readings.

What To Do Before We Arrive

  • If you can safely reach it, shut off the main water valve to stop the source if the damage is from an active leak or burst pipe rather than a one-time event like an overflow.
  • Move furniture, rugs, and electronics away from standing water where it is safe to do so. Do not use a household vacuum on standing water because of the electrical shock risk.
  • Photograph the damage before any cleanup begins. Insurance claims for water damage typically go smoother with dated photos of the affected area as you first found it.
  • Do not attempt to run a dehumidifier or fan for days on your own before extraction. Drying without first extracting standing water can push moisture deeper into flooring and walls.
