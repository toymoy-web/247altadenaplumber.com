Hydro jetting uses high-pressure water to scour the entire inside diameter of a drain or sewer line. It is a heavier-duty service than routine snaking, aimed at grease buildup, root intrusion, and lines that keep clogging in the same place.

When Hydro Jetting Makes Sense In Altadena

Restaurants and other commercial kitchens in the San Gabriel Valley generate grease that coats the inside of drain lines over time. A snake can punch through the middle of that buildup, but jetting is the only method that clears the full pipe wall.

On residential lines, jetting is most useful for mainlines with root intrusion from Altadena's mature trees, since it clears both the debris and much of the root mass in a single pass, buying more time before the next clog.

A line that has never been jetted often holds years of accumulated buildup even if it has never fully clogged, which is why some property managers schedule preventative jetting on a routine basis rather than waiting for a backup.

Signs A Line Needs Jetting, Not Just Snaking

Clogs Return Within Weeks

A drain that clogs again shortly after snaking usually has grease or scale coating the pipe walls that a snake did not remove.

Multiple Slow Drains

A main line with heavy buildup or root intrusion often shows up as several slow fixtures at once.

Known Grease Buildup

Kitchen lines in homes or commercial spaces with heavy cooking use are prone to grease-related buildup over time.

Camera-Confirmed Root Intrusion

A sewer camera inspection showing root mass inside the line is a direct indicator that jetting will outperform snaking.

How We Approach A Jetting Job

We run a sewer camera through the line first whenever possible, to confirm the type and location of the blockage before choosing jetting over a simpler snaking job.

The jetting equipment then scours the full pipe diameter with high-pressure water, clearing grease, scale, and root material that a mechanical snake can only partially displace.

How Do I Know If I Need Jetting Instead Of Snaking?

Jetting Vs. Snaking

Snaking

Faster and less expensive for a single, isolated clog with no significant buildup on the pipe walls.

Hydro Jetting

Better for grease-coated lines, recurring clogs, or root intrusion, since it clears the entire pipe diameter.

Camera-First Approach

Confirming the actual condition of the line before choosing a method avoids paying for jetting a line that only needed a simple snake.

Common Questions

Is hydro jetting safe for older pipes?

We assess pipe condition with a camera first. Jetting is safe for sound pipe but is not the right tool for a line already compromised by heavy corrosion or cracking, which needs repair instead.

How often does a commercial kitchen line need jetting?

It depends on volume and grease trap maintenance, but many commercial lines benefit from jetting on a routine schedule rather than waiting for a full clog.

Does jetting remove tree roots completely?

Jetting clears root mass from inside the pipe effectively, but if roots are entering through a crack or joint, the underlying pipe damage should be addressed separately to prevent regrowth.

Can hydro jetting damage older pipes?

Properly calibrated jetting is safe for sound pipe of any common residential material. The camera inspection beforehand is what protects against using it on a pipe too compromised to handle the pressure.

Before We Arrive

  • Note which drains are affected and how often they have clogged recently, since a pattern of recurring clogs is a strong signal jetting is the right call.
  • If this is a commercial kitchen line, have your grease trap pumping records available if you have them.
  • Avoid pouring chemical drain cleaners into a line you suspect needs jetting, since some chemicals can complicate the jetting process.
