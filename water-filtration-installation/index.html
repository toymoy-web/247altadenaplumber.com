Water filtration addresses taste, odor, chlorine, and sediment concerns, which is a different problem than water hardness and needs a different system than a softener.

Why Filtration Comes Up Across Altadena

Because Altadena draws from several different mutual water companies plus a section served by Pasadena Water and Power, taste and mineral content can genuinely differ block to block, which is part of why longtime residents sometimes have strong opinions about whose water tastes better.

Homes on older service lines sometimes notice sediment or discoloration after nearby utility work disturbs aging pipe, which a point-of-entry filter can address even when the underlying line itself is not being replaced right away.

A household coming back into a finished Eaton Fire rebuild sometimes notices a taste or sediment change simply because new plumbing is running through a service line that predates the rebuilt structure, which filtration can address without waiting on a service line replacement.

Signs Filtration Would Help

Chlorine Taste Or Smell

A noticeable chlorine taste is one of the most common reasons homeowners look into whole-home filtration.

Visible Sediment

Fine particles in a glass of water or in an aerator screen point to sediment that a filter can catch before it reaches fixtures.

Inconsistent Taste Between Faucets

Different fixtures pulling from different branches of aging plumbing can taste noticeably different.

Wanting To Reduce Bottled Water Use

A whole-home or under-sink system is a common alternative for households relying heavily on bottled water for taste reasons.

Choosing The Right System

Whole-home filtration treats water at the point it enters the house, addressing sediment and chlorine for every fixture, while an under-sink system targets just the kitchen tap for drinking and cooking water.

Filter media selected for a specific concern is matched against NSF/ANSI 42 (taste and odor reduction) or NSF/ANSI 53 (health-related contaminant reduction), the standard certifications that define what a given filter is actually rated to remove.

We identify the specific concern, taste, odor, sediment, or something else, before recommending a system, since a sediment-focused filter and a taste-focused carbon filter are not interchangeable.

System Options

Whole-Home Filtration

Treats all water entering the house, addressing sediment and chlorine at every fixture.

Under-Sink Filtration

Targets drinking and cooking water at a single tap, often paired with a dedicated faucet.

Combined With Softening

A household with both hardness and taste concerns can run a softener and a filtration system together.

Common Questions

What is the difference between a filter and a softener?

A softener removes hardness minerals like calcium and magnesium. A filter addresses taste, odor, chlorine, and sediment, which are separate concerns.

Do I need whole-home filtration or just under the kitchen sink?

If your only concern is drinking and cooking water taste, an under-sink system is often enough. Whole-home filtration makes more sense for sediment or chlorine affecting showers and laundry too.

How often do filtration systems need maintenance?

Most systems need periodic filter cartridge changes, with frequency depending on water usage and the specific system installed.

Can filtration remove all taste and odor issues?

A properly matched carbon-based filter resolves most chlorine taste and odor complaints, though a water quality test helps confirm the right filter media for a specific concern.

Does filtration also help with sediment after utility work nearby?

Yes. A sediment pre-filter ahead of any carbon stage is specifically effective at catching the fine particles that sometimes appear after nearby utility or repair work disturbs an aging line.

How is filtration priced compared to a softener?

Cost depends on whether you choose whole-home or under-sink coverage and the specific filter media needed. We provide a flat quote after confirming your water quality concern.
